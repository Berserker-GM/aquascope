## Summary

The screening result, run on the 95.7-year daily record at USGS-01646502 (Potomac River, adjusted, near Washington, DC, 1930-03-01 to 2025-10-31), shows the 8 m3/s abstraction can be met on 82.53% of days and 92.08% of annual volume once a 50.1 m3/s (Q95) reserve is kept in the channel and the take is capped at 10% of flow, requiring the river to carry at least 80 m3/s. Only 4.17% of years are fully free of shortfall, and the worst year (1965) has 195 short days. The river's mean flow (335.9 m3/s) and median (Q50, 200 m3/s) are far above demand, but the 7Q10 low flow is 31.68 m3/s and baseflow index 0.70, showing dry spells that fall below the reserve threshold. No trend is detected in the annual mean flow (Mann-Kendall p=0.5753, Sen's slope 0.2006 m3/s/yr).

## Problem and decision

The question is whether an 8 m3/s abstraction at Little Falls on the Potomac can be reliably supplied to the city's water works run-of-river, without storage, through a dry year, given the demand is fixed and municipal.

## Methodology

Four steps used the point's catchment and the closest long gauge, USGS-01646502 (Potomac River, adjusted, near Washington, DC), daily discharge 1930-03-01 to 2025-10-31 (95.7 years, 34940 daily values). First, catchment attributes were pulled from HydroATLAS (BasinATLAS) to check basin size and regulation. Second, the flow-duration curve and a Mann-Kendall trend test on annual means were computed at the gauge, alongside GEV (L-moments) and Log-Pearson III flood-frequency fits. Third, low-flow signatures (Q95, Q50, Q10, Q25, Q75, Q90, baseflow index via Lyne-Hollick filter, and 7Q10 via Weibull plotting position) were derived from the same record. Fourth, a run-of-river supply-reliability screening applied the 8 m3/s demand against the daily series, keeping Q95 (50.1 m3/s) in the river and capping the abstraction at 10% of daily flow, following flow-duration-curve environmental-flow screening practice (Vogel and Fennessey 1994; Smakhtin and Eriyagama 2008; Acreman and Dunbar 2004).

## Limitations and what this study does not establish

This is a screening rule, not a licence assessment: keeping Q95 (50.1 m3/s) in the river and capping the take at 10% of flow follows flow-duration-curve environmental-flow practice (Vogel and Fennessey 1994; Smakhtin and Eriyagama 2008; Acreman and Dunbar 2004), not a regulator's flow standard for the Potomac. Upstream abstractions, return flows and any storage operations above the gauge -- including the 297 million m3 of upstream reservoir volume and 2.6% degree of regulation noted at the catchment scale -- are not represented in the reliability number. The reliability figures describe the 1930-2025 record; a changing climate, new upstream abstraction, or a drier decade than any recorded would move both the daily and annual reliability estimates. No cause is established for the (statistically insignificant) trend in annual mean flow. The catalog does not confirm daily resolution explicitly, though the sampling rate (365.1 values/year) is consistent with it.

## Caveats

- A screening rule, not a licence assessment: Q95 kept in the river and at most the stated share of the flow taken are assumptions in the tradition of flow-duration-curve environmental-flow practice (Smakhtin and Eriyagama 2008; Acreman and Dunbar 2004); the regulator's flow standard, return flows, upstream abstractions and storage are not in the number.
- Reliability read off the record describes the years on record; a changing climate, new upstream abstraction or a drier decade than any recorded moves it.

## Recommendations

On the numbers obtained, an 8 m3/s abstraction at Little Falls should be adopted only with the understanding that it is reliable on about 82.53% of days and 92.08% of annual volume under the Q95-reserve, 10%-share screening rule, but that only about 4.17% of years are free of any shortfall day, with up to 195 short days in the worst year (1965) -- this does not carry a recommendation of unconditional run-of-river reliability in a dry year. Adoption should be conditioned on a firm environmental-flow standard from the regulator (in place of the Q95 screening default), on quantifying upstream abstractions and any storage releases above the gauge, and on a mechanism (curtailment, alternative source, or storage) for the days flow falls below the 80 m3/s threshold. To firm up the figure, obtain the regulator's actual minimum-flow condition, upstream abstraction and return-flow data, and a check of the low-flow record against the 2025-2026 period beyond the catalog's stated end date.
